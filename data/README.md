# Data sets of the benchmark study

The open-data study compares the kernel Poisson estimator with its competitors on ten public count-regression data sets. They range from 147 to 678013 observations and cover the situations the method is meant for: a designed experiment, panels and portfolios with exposures, daily and hourly time series, software defects and a small social-science sample. The module `datasets.py` obtains each one from its public source, checks it, and returns it in one common form: `load(name)` gives the covariates `X` (a pandas DataFrame whose factor columns hold strings), the counts `y`, the exposures (or `None` for unit exposure), the names of the factor columns, the group labels used for grouped or blocked cross-validation (or `None`), and a one-sentence description, the source, the licence and the full reference of the data's origin. The function `describe` returns the statistics of the table below, and `fremtpl2_split` the fixed split of freMTPL2 described further down.

## How the files are obtained and checked

Every raw file is downloaded once into `cache/NAME/`, a folder that git ignores: nothing from the cache is ever committed, which also respects the one licence (Eclipse) that forbids redistribution. Each download first lands in a new, empty folder of its own, where its format is checked (a zip or tar archive, an ARFF header, a CSV header rather than an error page) and, for the two OpenML files, its MD5 is compared with the value OpenML publishes; only then is it moved into the cache. The SHA256 of every file is recorded in `checksums.json`, which belongs to this repository: each download and every later load is compared with it, and a file that differs is refused, so that all results are computed from byte-identical inputs. Two data sets pass through R: the Chicago series is an `.rda` file inside the source package of dlnm, extracted alone into an isolated folder and converted to CSV by `export_rdata.R` under `Rscript --vanilla`, and the bioChemists sample is exported by the same script from the installed package pscl. These derived CSV files are recorded and checked in the same way.

No covariate is a function of the response: densities, rates, sub-counts, claim indicators, claim amounts and the components of a total are always removed, and the tests of `tests/test_data_and_baselines.py` check this for every data set.

## The data sets

**fishing.** The total number of demersal fish (`totabund`) caught by bottom trawl at 147 sites between 804 and 4865 m depth in the north-east Atlantic, in 1977 to 1989 and 2000 to 2002, from the study of Bailey, Collins, Gordon, Zuur and Priede (2009) on the deep reach of fisheries, as adapted by Zuur, Hilbe and Ieno (2013) and distributed in the R package COUNT. The exposure is the swept area of the trawl (`sweptarea`). The covariates are the mean depth and the year (continuous) and the period (a factor). The period is a step function of the year; both are kept, so that a linear model sees the jump between the two periods and a flexible model also the trend within them. The column `density`, which is `totabund` divided by `sweptarea`, and the site identifier are removed. The file is the CSV of Rdatasets (COUNT/fishing), under the GPL-2 licence of COUNT. The documentation of COUNT dates the article 2008 with volume 275; it appeared in volume 276 (2009) of the Proceedings of the Royal Society B, doi:10.1098/rspb.2009.0098.

**solder.** The number of visible solder skips on printed-circuit boards (`skips`) in a designed experiment on wave soldering reported by Comizzoli, Landwehr and Sinclair (1990, AT&T Technical Journal 69(6), 113–128) and published in Chambers and Hastie (1992, Statistical Models in S). All five covariates are factors: the clearance around the mounting pad (`Opening`, 3 levels), the thickness of the solder (`Solder`, 2), the solder mask (`Mask`, 5), the geometry and size of the pad (`PadType`, 10) and the panel of the board (`Panel`, 3). There is no exposure. The file is the CSV of Rdatasets (rpart/solder, 900 rows), under the GPL-2 or GPL-3 licence of rpart.

**fatalities.** The annual number of traffic fatalities (`fatal`, from the Fatal Accident Reporting System of the US Department of Transportation) in the 48 contiguous states from 1982 to 1988, the panel of Ruhm (1996, Journal of Health Economics 15(4), 435–454) as distributed with Stock and Watson (2007, Introduction to Econometrics) in the R package AER. The exposure is the total vehicle-miles travelled (`milestot`, in millions of miles). The thirteen continuous covariates are the beer tax, the consumption of spirits, the unemployment rate, the income per capita, the ratio of employment to population, the share of young drivers, the average miles per driver, the shares of Southern Baptists and of Mormons, the share of the population living in dry counties, the minimum legal drinking age (fractional in the years a new age was phased in), the growth rate of the gross state product, and the year; the three factors are the laws on preliminary breath tests, mandatory jail and mandatory community service. The other fatality counts (night-time, single-vehicle, by age group, alcohol-involved) are parts of the response and are removed, as are the populations and the two national series (unemployment and employment ratio of the United States), which are functions of the year. The one state-year that lacks the two sentencing laws (California, 1988) is dropped, which leaves 335 of the 336 rows. The group is the state, so that grouped cross-validation predicts whole states. The file is the CSV of Rdatasets (AER/Fatalities), under the GPL-2 or GPL-3 licence of AER.

**abalone.** The number of shell rings (`rings`, from 1 to 29; the age in years is the number of rings plus 1.5) of 4177 blacklip abalone from the north coast and islands of Bass Strait, Tasmania (Nash, Sellers, Talbot, Cawthorn and Ford, 1994, Sea Fisheries Division Technical Report 48). The covariates are the sex (a factor with levels F, M and I for infant) and seven physical measurements: length, diameter and height in millimetres, and the whole, shucked, viscera and shell weights in grams. OpenML divided every measurement by 200; the loader multiplies them back, which changes nothing for methods that standardise their inputs. There is no exposure. The file is the ARFF file of OpenML data set 44956 (its MD5 checked against OpenML's), a copy of UCI Machine Learning Repository data set 1 (doi:10.24432/C55C7W), under the CC BY 4.0 licence.

**chicago.** The daily number of deaths from all causes except accidents (`death`) in Chicago from 1987 to 2000, from the National Morbidity, Mortality and Air Pollution Study (Samet, Dominici, Zeger, Schwartz and Dockery, 2000, Health Effects Institute Research Report 94, Part I) as distributed in the data set `chicagoNMMAPS` of the R package dlnm (Gasparrini, 2011, Journal of Statistical Software 43(8), doi:10.18637/jss.v043.i08). The continuous covariates are the mean temperature (degrees Celsius), the dew point temperature, the PM10 and ozone concentrations (series reconstructed by dlnm from de-trended values, hence a few slightly negative values), the day index and the day of the year; the day of the week is a factor. The cardiovascular and respiratory death counts are parts of the response and are removed. The relative humidity is missing on every day of 1998 to 2000 and is nearly a function of the temperature and the dew point, so it is left out; the 251 days without a PM10 value are dropped, which leaves 4863 of the 5114 days. The group is the calendar year, so that blocked cross-validation predicts whole years. The file is the source package `dlnm_2.4.10.tar.gz` from CRAN (or from the CRAN archive once a newer version replaces it), of which only `data/chicagoNMMAPS.rda` is extracted; the licence is GPL-2 or GPL-3 (dlnm).

**eclipse.** The number of post-release defects (`post`, defects fixed in the six months after the release) of each of the 10593 Java files of Eclipse 3.0, from release 2.0a of the Eclipse bug data of Zimmermann, Premraj and Zeller (2007, Predicting defects for Eclipse, PROMISE'07, doi:10.1109/PROMISE.2007.10), file level, in `promise-2_0a-files-csv.zip`. The exposure is the number of lines of code of the file (`TLOC`, from 3 to 4886). The covariates are the 30 code metrics of the data aggregated over the methods or classes of the file by their average, maximum and sum (method calls `FOUT`, method lines of code `MLOC`, nested block depth `NBD`, fields `NOF`, methods `NOM`, static fields `NSF`, static methods `NSM`, parameters `PAR`, cyclomatic complexity `VG`) together with the anonymous type declarations `ACD`, the interfaces `NOI` and the classes `NOT` of the file, each entered as log(1 + value) because the raw metrics are heavily skewed. The pre-release defect count (a measure of the development process rather than of the code), the counts of abstract-syntax-tree nodes, the plug-in and the file name are left out. The loader tries the server of Saarland University that hosts the data first, and takes the copy kept by the Internet Archive on 2023-03-07 when the server does not answer or serves a file whose SHA256 differs from the record; the recorded file is the one of the Internet Archive. The providers state that the factual data may be used without restriction but that the archive may not be redistributed: it is downloaded and never committed.

**bike.** The hourly number of rented bicycles (`cnt`) of the Capital Bikeshare system of Washington D.C. in 2011 and 2012, 17379 hours, from Fanaee-T and Gama (2014, Progress in Artificial Intelligence 2(2–3), 113–127, doi:10.1007/s13748-013-0040-3), file `hour.csv` of UCI Machine Learning Repository data set 275 (doi:10.24432/C5W894), under the CC BY 4.0 licence. The continuous covariates are the hour of the day, its cyclic encoding by sin(2π hour/24) and cos(2π hour/24), the temperature, the feeling temperature, the humidity and the wind speed (normalised as in the source); the factors are the day of the week, the working day, the holiday, the season, the weather situation and the year. The year is added to the list of covariates because the ridership grew by 63 per cent from 2011 to 2012, a level shift that no other covariate carries; the month is left out, the season and the temperature carrying the annual cycle. The counts of casual and of registered users sum to the response and are removed. The group is the day (731 days), so that blocked cross-validation never splits the hours of one day between training and test.

**datacar.** The number of claims (`numclaims`, from 0 to 4) of 67856 one-year vehicle insurance policies taken out in 2004 or 2005, the data of de Jong and Heller (2008, Generalized Linear Models for Insurance Data, Cambridge University Press, doi:10.1017/CBO9780511755408) as distributed in the R package insuranceData. The exposure is the insured fraction of the year (from 0.0027 to 0.9993). The covariates are the vehicle value (continuous, in tens of thousands of dollars) and five factors: the body type (13 levels), the age band of the vehicle (4), the gender of the driver (2), the area (6) and the age band of the driver (6); the two ordered bands are treated as factors, as by de Jong and Heller. The claim indicator `clm` and the claim cost `claimcst0` are functions of the response and are removed, as is the constant column `X_OBSTAT_`. The file is the CSV of Rdatasets (insuranceData/dataCar), under the GPL-2 licence of insuranceData.

**fremtpl2.** The number of claims (`ClaimNb`) of 678013 French motor third-party liability policies, observed mostly over one year, the data set freMTPL2freq of the R package CASdatasets (Dutang and Charpentier; Charpentier, 2014, Computational Actuarial Science with R, CRC Press), file of OpenML data set 41214 in ARFF format (its MD5 checked against OpenML's). The exposure is the insured period in years. Following Noll, Salzmann and Wüthrich (2018, Case Study: French Motor Third-Party Liability Claims, doi:10.2139/ssrn.3164764), the counts are capped at 4 (9 policies have between 5 and 16 claims), the exposures at 1 year (1224 policies exceed it, up to 2.01), the vehicle age at 20 years (8322 policies), the age of the driver at 90 years (401) and the bonus-malus level at 150 (209); the population density of the driver's town enters through its logarithm. The continuous covariates are the vehicle power, the vehicle age, the age of the driver, the bonus-malus level and the log density; the factors are the area code (6 levels), the vehicle brand (11), the fuel (2) and the region (22). The policy identifier `IDpol` is not a covariate. The rows are shuffled by a fixed permutation (seed 20261018) and the original row of each one is kept in `row_id`. The function `fremtpl2_split` returns the positions, in the shuffled rows, of the training (454268) and test (223745) policies of the scikit-learn example on Poisson regression, `train_test_split(test_size=0.33, random_state=0)` applied to the rows in their original order. OpenML distributes the file under CC0; CASdatasets itself is under the GPL (⩾ 2).

**biochemists.** The number of articles (`art`) published in the last three years of the doctorate by 915 biochemistry doctoral students, from Long (1990, The origins of sex differences in science, Social Forces 68(4), 1297–1316, doi:10.2307/2579146) and Long (1997, Regression Models for Categorical and Limited Dependent Variables, Sage). The factors are the gender and the marital status; the continuous covariates are the number of children aged five or less, the prestige of the doctoral department and the number of articles of the mentor. The data set is exported by R from the installed package pscl (`data(bioChemists, package = "pscl")`, GPL-2); R with the package pscl is therefore needed for this data set, as for Chicago. The documentation of pscl gives issue 3 of Social Forces 68; the article is in issue 4.
